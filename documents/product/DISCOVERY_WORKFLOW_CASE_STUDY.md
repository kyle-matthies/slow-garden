## Why this case matters

A person has assembled a workflow that helps her discover what she is trying to think, connect it to other ideas, and bring that understanding into a real decision. Her tools are notes, an AI agent, deliberate exploration, and eventually a reusable skill. The product-relevant experience is the movement from scattered resonance to a personally meaningful question and a different way of approaching a choice.

This is a concrete example of the discovery Slow Garden aims to support. It is evidence of one creator's described practice and immediate reactions. It does not establish broad demand, durable improvement in judgment, willingness to pay, or the advantage of delayed AI over live conversation.

## The user and the job

**Situation:** A reflective creator has accumulated personally meaningful notes, but cannot reliably bring those ideas into decisions when they matter.

**Tension:** She wants ambitious, effective learning experiences that retain generosity and play. She experiences some of these values as competing and is looking for a way to hold them together.

**Working job statement, inferred from the transcript:** When ideas have resonated with me but I cannot yet explain their relationship, help me discover the question connecting them, explore perspectives that deepen it, and use that understanding in a situation I care about.

**Current baseline:** Obsidian or another notes store plus a general AI agent, with the person selecting material, prompting, judging responses, retrieving additional notes, and calibrating a reusable skill.

The administrative work includes routing context, asking for patterns, requesting a different domain, and preserving a useful result. The intellectual work includes choosing what matters, recognizing or rejecting a framing, bringing an older thought back into view, and deciding what the connection changes. A Slow Garden translation should reduce the former while preserving the latter.

These stages form a loop. A proposed connection can send the person back to earlier notes; a real decision can expose missing context; an alternative phrasing can change the inquiry itself. A rigid wizard would miss that movement.

## What makes this discovery rather than a summary

**The person recognizes a question that was implicit in her selections.** The useful output is about the relationship between the ideas she noticed and the work she is trying to do. It remains a proposed interpretation, not a definitive account of her personality.

**Different framings reveal different implications.** During deliberate exploration, she finds value in comparing nearby lenses. This supports offering room to reconsider an interpretation. It does not support random regeneration, unstable factual claims, or unsolicited repeated returns from unchanged material.

**A connection travels outward and then returns to personal context.** Hospitality leads to philosophical questions; a discussion of care reminds her of her own earlier reading. The creator supplies that second connection. The transcript does not demonstrate an agent independently searching her entire note collection and discovering Heidegger.

**The connection changes a question she can act on.** Her initial choice concerns course duration. The reframing asks what participants need to become able to do, and which parts require sustained time. That gives her a way to examine the perceived trade-off. It does not prove both outcomes can be achieved within her actual constraints.

**The useful moment precedes the saved artifact.** She reports a shift around 24:20–24:57 and describes another in the supplied narration around 33:10–34:12. Creating the skill preserves a way back to that thinking. Skill creation is not the only possible destination or the necessary success event.

## Translation into Slow Garden

These are research hypotheses within the [product framing](PRODUCT_FRAMING.md), [trust contract](TRUST_AND_APPROVAL_CONTRACT.md), and current [ADR-006](../architecture/ADR-006_WEB_THINKING_GARDEN.md). They do not change implementation scope.

| Experience to preserve | Possible Slow Garden expression | Boundary to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Personally meaningful fragments accumulate | Dated entries develop within named seeds and plots | Writing must remain useful and uninterrupted without AI |
| An implicit question becomes legible | A small return offers a tentative connection or tension with exact supporting revisions | The user owns the interpretation and can correct or decline it |
| Older thoughts acquire new relevance | A return connects material within an authorized plot or across explicitly participating plots | Similarity never grants permission; current plot rules govern |
| A connection raises curiosity | One question invites the person to add a thought in their own words | No forced conversation, obligation to reply, or completion pressure |
| A perspective becomes useful in a decision | The return helps separate desired outcomes from assumed solutions | The system supports judgment and does not decide for the person |
| A useful lens can be revisited | A kept return and subsequent human entry retain the thread and its evidence | Acceptance does not rewrite sources or create a task automatically |

The design tension is important: this creator works through a synchronous conversation. Slow Garden protects quiet capture and offers a manual invitation followed by a return when ready, with scheduling still gated. The research question is whether sufficient personal context and a well-composed return can preserve discovery without requiring all of the conversational steering. No artificial delay is implied by this case.

External philosophy research and reusable agent skills belong to later, separately gated exploration and workbench questions. The first useful translation can use only material the person has already written and authorized for a pass.

## Illustrative return to test

The following is authored for this case study. It is not a live bloom, an actual extraction from the creator's private notes, or approved product copy. A working system would need exact source revisions before displaying it.

> **What would let both outcomes survive?**
>
> Your notes on learning design emphasize an early sense of progress. Your hospitality notes explore how care and delight can coexist with disciplined operations. There may be a shared question here: which parts of the experience need time, and which can create a meaningful first win immediately?

The initial return could remain this small. Evidence inspection would distinguish the person's entries, quoted or paraphrased source material, and the proposed relationship. The person could keep, correct, or prune it, then write what it brings to mind. A response might introduce a new constraint or reject the relationship entirely; either can be useful thinking.

The mechanism to test is: accumulated personal context → tentative relationship → human recognition or correction → a new question or articulation → further thought. That can fit within one bloom. It does not require three outputs simply because three are allowed.

## How to use this case in research

Link this case to existing R-001, R-002, R-004, R-005, and R-008; it does not complete them. Use it to sharpen the existing diary and comparison studies, not to add an unrelated study program.

- **Compare the same consented material:** quiet writing without AI, a conventional summary, an explicit general-agent conversation, and a bounded Slow Garden return. An ablation can compare one entry with the accumulated set to examine what accumulation contributes. Timing should be varied separately from context and interface where feasible.
- **Look for a person-owned change:** can the person explain a previously unnamed tension, identify a useful connection, or formulate a different question in their own words? Later follow-up can ask whether this mattered in an actual choice.
- **Check faithfulness and agency:** can the person inspect the evidence, distinguish interpretation from source, disagree, and continue thinking without adopting the system's framing?
- **Measure the cost of the return:** include reading burden, unwanted steering, emotional overreach, and whether a question feels inviting or like homework. Approval clicks and output counts are weak substitutes for this evidence.
- **Keep negative controls:** sparse notes, unrelated notes, genuine resource trade-offs, tempting but unsupported analogies, and relevant material in a plot that has not permitted cross-pollination. Zero blooms remains a valid result.
- **Retain stop conditions:** if the return is an attractive paraphrase, if new vocabulary obscures the original problem, if false connections dominate, or if a simple notes-plus-agent workflow provides equivalent value with less effort, revise the hypothesis.

The strongest contribution of this case is a concrete target for the experience: a person can see how their own thoughts relate, discover something they want to investigate, and remain the author of what happens next.
